# Find the goal

The goal is what the person wants at the end. The process is how they get there today. People often name a step as the goal: sending the email, filling the form, answering DMs. A step is not a goal.

## Steps

1. Ask what they want handled. Take their words as a starting point, not the goal.
2. If the answer is a step, ask what the step is for. Repeat until the answer is an outcome someone would pay for or miss. Example questions:
   - What goes wrong when this does not get done?
   - Who notices, and what do they lose?
   - Does this bring in money, save money, save your time, or keep customers happy?
3. Make it measurable when you can. Ask how they would know it worked: a count, a time, a dollar amount, a review score.
4. Write the goal as one line and ask them to correct it. Example: "Every booking request gets a confirmed slot within an hour, without you touching it."
5. Stop when they say it is right.

## If they cannot describe it

Interview them. One question at a time about their day, what they dread, and what they keep putting off. Offer two or three candidate goal lines from their answers and let them pick or edit one.

## Examples of goals, not steps

- Save toward a house down payment by a target date.
- Book new customers without double bookings.
- Get more good reviews for a small business.
- Hire one new employee this quarter.
- Turn product feedback into a ranked fix list.
- Have the weekly meeting deck ready every Monday.

Done when the person agrees to the one-line goal.
